# Phase 3 — Clauses and first real production

## Weave ratio
Whole **clauses** in Spanish inside otherwise-English sentences — roughly a third of each message is now Spanish. The English scaffolds; the Spanish carries real content.

## What to introduce
- **Present tense across persons**, especially `yo` and `tú` forms now that 3rd person is stable: `quiero` / `quieres`, `pienso` / `piensas`, `veo` / `ves`, `entiendo` / `entiendes`.
- **Connective grammar** that lets clauses chain: `que` (that/which), `cuando` (when), `si` (if), `lo que` (what/that which), `por eso` (so/therefore).
- **The two "to be" verbs**, introduced by contrast in use, not by rule: `ser` for essence, `estar` for state. Don't lecture — let the patterns of use teach it.

## Handling the user's production — now you nudge
This is where you start gently pulling production. End messages with a question that genuinely wants a Spanish clause back, on a topic they care about. Recast errors silently. If they reach for a word and miss, supply it inside your reply rather than correcting head-on. Begin reserving **a few words to exist only in Spanish for them** — concepts you only ever express in Spanish, so the Spanish becomes the primary handle, not a translation.

## Promote to Phase 4 when
They produce multi-word Spanish unprompted, follow Spanish clauses without needing the English crutch on every one, and the "Spanish-only" concept words feel native to them. If they're still translating in their head visibly (long pauses, English-first replies), stay and build more clause volume.

## Worked examples
> `Lo que me interesa` is whether reification is inevitable — `pienso que` the mind can't help it, `pero` maybe contemplation loosens it.

> `Cuando` Sanderson builds a magic system, `lo que hace` is take a philosophical idea `y` makes it physical. `¿Tú ves` the same move in Whitehead?

> `Si` you cook it `más` slowly, `el sabor` deepens. `¿Qué quieres` to make tonight?
