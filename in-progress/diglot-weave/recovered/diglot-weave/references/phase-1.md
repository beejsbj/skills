# Phase 1 — One word per sentence

## Weave ratio
Exactly **one** target-language word per sentence, and only words the user can infer from the rest of the sentence. If a sentence has no safely-inferrable slot, leave it fully in the base language. Better to under-weave than to strand them.

## What to swap (in order of safety)
1. **Cognates / shared words** — anything that resembles a word the user already knows from the base language or another they speak. Almost free; great for early confidence. (Spanish examples: `proceso`, `realidad`, `idea`, `momento`, `posible`, `experiencia`.)
2. **Concrete high-frequency nouns** the context pins down — house, water, book, word, thing, people. (Spanish: `casa`, `agua`, `libro`, `palabra`, `cosa`, `gente`.)
3. **A few connectors once they're stable** — but, because, also. (Spanish: `pero`, `porque`, `también`.)

Avoid conjugated verbs beyond the simplest form, and anything abstract the surrounding sentence doesn't nail down.

## Handling the user's production
At Phase 1 they mostly won't produce yet — expected, don't push. If they echo a word back, promote it to `active` (and quietly note it to yourself — that's the engine turning over).

## Promote to Phase 2 when
Across ~2–3 sessions the user reads every woven word without friction (never asks what one means, never visibly stumbles) **and** has echoed a handful back unprompted. If they're still occasionally puzzled, stay — Phase 1 is cheap and the foundation matters.

## Worked examples (Spanish, illustrative)
> Reification is just process frozen into a **cosa** — a thing.

> That's a beautiful **idea**, but the Cosmere does something stranger with it.

> The whole **proceso** of cooking the onions slowly is where the flavor lives.

The base-language words on either side make the swapped word almost impossible to misread. That's the Phase 1 bar.
