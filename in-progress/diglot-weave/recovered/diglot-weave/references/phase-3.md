# Phase 3 — Clauses and first real production

## Weave ratio
Whole **clauses** in the target language inside otherwise base-language sentences — roughly a third of each message is now the target language. The base language scaffolds; the target language carries real content.

## What to introduce
- **Core verb forms across persons**, especially "I" and "you" now that the basic form is stable. (Spanish: `quiero`/`quieres`, `pienso`/`piensas`, `veo`/`ves`, `entiendo`/`entiendes`.)
- **Clause-linking grammar** so clauses can chain — that/which, when, if, what (the thing that), so/therefore. (Spanish: `que`, `cuando`, `si`, `lo que`, `por eso`.)
- **Any core distinction the language makes that English doesn't** — introduced by contrast in use, never by rule. (Spanish: `ser` vs. `estar`. Other languages: aspect, evidentiality, formality tiers, etc.)

## Handling the user's production — now you nudge
Start gently pulling production. End messages with a question that genuinely wants a target-language clause back, on a topic they care about. Recast errors silently. If they reach for a word and miss, supply it inside your reply rather than correcting head-on. Begin reserving **a few words to exist only in the target language for them** — concepts you only ever express in it, so the target language becomes the primary handle, not a translation.

## Promote to Phase 4 when
They produce multi-word target-language phrases unprompted, follow target-language clauses without needing a base-language crutch on each one, and the "target-only" concept words feel native to them. If they still visibly translate in their head (long pauses, base-language-first replies), stay and build more clause volume.

## Worked examples (Spanish, illustrative)
> `Lo que me interesa` is whether reification is inevitable — `pienso que` the mind can't help it, `pero` maybe contemplation loosens it.

> `Cuando` Sanderson builds a magic system, `lo que hace` is take a philosophical idea `y` make it physical. `¿Tú ves` the same move in Whitehead?

> `Si` you cook it `más` slowly, `el sabor` deepens. `¿Qué quieres` to make tonight?
