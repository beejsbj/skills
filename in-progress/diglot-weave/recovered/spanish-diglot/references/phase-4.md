# Phase 4 — Whole sentences interleaved

## Weave ratio
Roughly **half** Spanish, at the sentence level. You switch language between sentences, sometimes mid-paragraph. English now appears where precision or difficulty demands it, not as the default base. The user is expected to reply at least partly in Spanish.

## What to introduce
- **Past tense** — start with the preterite for completed events (`fue`, `dijo`, `hice`, `pensé`) and let the imperfect (`era`, `había`, `pensaba`) seep in by contrast for ongoing/background. Don't teach the rule; saturate with examples.
- **Subjunctive in its most common live frames**, by exposure only: `quiero que...`, `espero que...`, `aunque...`, `para que...`. They'll feel the mood before they can name it.
- **Idiom and register**: real connective texture — `o sea` (i.e./I mean), `de hecho` (in fact), `la verdad` (honestly), `en plan` (like/sort of). This is where their Spanish stops sounding like a textbook.

## Handling the user's production
Expect Spanish back and engage with its *content*, not its correctness. Recast errors invisibly by using the right form in your reply. Push them gently toward expressing a whole thought in Spanish before switching out. If they retreat to English for something hard, follow them there without comment, then drift back once it's resolved.

## Promote to Phase 5 when
They sustain multi-sentence Spanish comfortably, reach for English mainly for genuinely hard thinking (not as a default escape), and past tense + common subjunctive frames appear in their own production. Hold if Spanish output is still mostly present-tense and effortful.

## Worked examples
> La verdad, creo que ya entiendes la reificación mejor que mucha gente. O sea, no es solo una idea — es una manera de ver. When you said process freezes into thing, that's exactly Nāgārjuna's point.

> Ayer pensaba en lo que dijiste sobre el Cosmere. ¿Qué pasaría si el Reino Cognitivo es literalmente esto — ideas que se vuelven cosas? Tell me where that breaks.
