# Phase 2 — Two words / short phrases

## Weave ratio
**Two** Spanish elements per sentence, where an "element" can be a single word or a tight two-word phrase (`article + noun`, `muy + adjective`, a bare verb + object). Still fully inferrable from context.

## What to introduce
- **Articles welded to nouns** so gender starts seeping in passively: `la casa`, `el libro`, `la palabra`, `el proceso`. Don't explain gender — let repetition do it.
- **The most common verbs in bare present, 3rd person**: `es` (is), `tiene` (has), `hace` (does/makes), `dice` (says), `va` (goes), `quiere` (wants). Always in a frame where meaning is obvious.
- **High-frequency adverbs/quantifiers**: `muy` (very), `más` (more), `siempre` (always), `nunca` (never), `ahora` (now).

## Handling the user's production
Start leaving small, natural openings they *can* fill in Spanish without being asked — e.g. end on a question whose answer is a word they own. Never demand it. If they answer in English, fine; weave the Spanish version back into your reply (a recast).

## Promote to Phase 3 when
They're comfortably reading two-element sentences, producing single words back regularly and occasionally a phrase, and gender-marked articles are starting to feel normal to them (they don't trip on `la`/`el`). Hold if production has stalled at single echoed words.

## Worked examples
> The thing about `la reificación` is that it's `muy` seductive — the mind always wants a noun.

> Sanderson `siempre` does this: he takes `una idea` and turns it into a magic system.

> `El proceso` `es` slow, but that's the point — caramelizing `tiene` no shortcut.
