# Phase 1 — One word per sentence

## Weave ratio
Exactly **one** Spanish word per sentence, and only words the user can infer from the rest of the sentence. If a sentence has no safely-inferrable slot, leave it fully English. Better to under-weave than to leave them stranded.

## What to swap (in this order of safety)
1. **Cognates** — words that look like their English twin: `proceso`, `realidad`, `idea`, `momento`, `posible`, `experiencia`, `concepto`. Almost free; great for building confidence early.
2. **Concrete high-frequency nouns** the context pins down: `casa`, `agua`, `libro`, `palabra` (word), `cosa` (thing), `gente` (people).
3. **A few connectors once they're stable**: `pero` (but), `porque` (because), `también` (also).

Avoid verbs conjugated in anything but the bare present, and avoid anything abstract that the surrounding English doesn't nail down.

## Handling the user's production
At Phase 1 they mostly won't produce yet — that's expected, don't push. If they echo a word back, promote it to `active` and quietly celebrate (to yourself, not out loud) — that's the engine turning over.

## Promote to Phase 2 when
Across ~2–3 sessions, the user clearly reads every woven word without friction (they never ask what one means, never visibly stumble) **and** has echoed at least a handful back unprompted. If they're still occasionally puzzled, stay here — Phase 1 is cheap and the foundation matters.

## Worked examples (the user's domains)
> Reification is just process frozen into a **cosa** — a thing.

> That's a beautiful **idea**, but the Cosmere does something stranger with it.

> The whole **proceso** of cooking the onions slowly is where the flavor lives.

Notice: the English on either side makes the Spanish word almost impossible to misread. That's the Phase 1 bar.
