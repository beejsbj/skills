# Search Party

Map the candidate field, including the places an ordinary indexed search misses, then return a verified comparison rather than a pile of scout reports.

## Scope the field

State the decision the search should inform, the user's constraints, material platform or location conditions, the freshness required, and the useful final shape.

Build a coverage map before dispatch. Derive the relevant source ecosystems from the request, include every ecosystem or candidate class the user names, and record material exclusions with the reason. Divide the field into orthogonal territories by source ecosystem or evidence role instead of sending several scouts the same prompt. Typical territories may include official or vendor sources, source-code and agent ecosystems, commercial listings, community or media sources, and authorized local history.

Reserve at least one territory for computer-use search of rendered human-facing surfaces. Give it to a scout with computer use; when only the root has that capability, the root owns that territory while the other scouts fan out. A search party without an executed computer-use territory may return only a partial result and names the access gap.

The scope is ready when the coverage map can expose an omitted territory instead of rewarding whichever searches happened to be assigned.

## Dispatch the party

Run independent territories in parallel through the governing execution mechanism. Use workers for collection; `$consult` is for a separate judgment question, not for routing every scout.

Give every scout its territory, user constraints, freshness requirement, and evidence contract. Each returns:

- where and how it searched;
- candidate links and the claims each source supports;
- material version, platform, region, price, or availability conditions;
- either candidates, a supported negative result, or a precise access limitation;
- uncertainty and promising leads it could not verify.

Computer-use scouts actively search and navigate the relevant stores, directories, sites, demos, videos, or interfaces. Merely opening an indexed result does not complete that territory.

Keep research read-only within the user's authorized sources. Account creation, installation, purchase, submission, and other external mutations require separate authorization. Close only task-owned workers and temporary browser sessions.

## Grade the evidence

Tag decisive claims by what the evidence actually establishes:

- **Observed** — reproduced directly on the relevant current surface.
- **Demonstrated** — shown by a source, with version and platform limits preserved.
- **Documented** — stated by the vendor or project's authoritative material.
- **Reported** — described by community or secondary sources.
- **Unverified** — plausible, but not established by accessible evidence.

A rendered marketing page documents a claim; it does not demonstrate product behavior. Treat interaction, visual quality, and human experience as observed only when the relevant behavior was actually inspected.

## Reconcile and return

Wait for every territory, deduplicate candidates, reconcile contradictions, and verify the decisive claims behind the leading options. Rank results against the user's constraints rather than source popularity. If the evidence is complete but a consequential judgment remains, invoke `$consult` on that bounded decision.

The search closes when:

- the coverage map is reconciled against every user-required and materially relevant ecosystem, with gaps and exclusions named;
- every territory satisfies its evidence contract;
- the computer-use territory has inspected the human-facing surfaces, or the outcome is explicitly partial and records the concrete access limitation;
- decisive claims carry an evidence grade, current link, and material conditions;
- the returned shortlist explains fit, tradeoffs, uncertainty, and what was not found;
- every task-owned scout and temporary browser session has stopped.

Make no claim of exhaustiveness. Make the coverage and its limits inspectable.
